# Hooks

The first 1 to 3 seconds decide whether anyone hears the rest. Built mainly from vyralcontent's viral-hooks, adapted to a person speaking in their own voice.

## Three layers

1. **Verbal**: the first spoken line. 5 to 10 words, said in under ~2 seconds.
2. **Visual**: what's on screen in the first frame. Action already in progress, one obvious subject, something unfinished. The viewer should form a concrete question ("what happened?", "how?"). Skip establishing shots and walk-ins.
3. **Text**: 3 to 7 words on screen for viewers with sound off. It sharpens the verbal line rather than repeating it: drop verbs and articles, keep numbers and contrast.

Write the verbal line first, then the visual, then the text. All three must promise the same thing. In voiceover-only or audio formats, the verbal layer carries everything.

## Mechanisms

Offer options from different mechanisms, labeled, not rewordings of one. The shapes below are in English; write the real lines in the script's language and the speaker's voice.

| Mechanism | How it works | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curiosity gap (shaped) | Name the category of the answer, withhold the answer | "I tried every cold brew maker under 50 dollars. One actually works." |
| Contrarian | Contradict a belief the audience really holds | "Morning routines are why you're tired." |
| Mistake / stakes | An error the viewer is probably making, or what they lose | "This clause in your lease can cost you a month's rent." |
| Specific result | A concrete outcome with a real number | "I cut my grocery bill by a third with one rule." |
| Cost confession | What it cost the speaker so the viewer doesn't pay | "I wasted six months on this so you don't have to." |
| Experimenter | The speaker tested something | "I did X for 30 days to see if Y." |
| Investigator | A hidden thing in a familiar place | "There's a setting on your phone that's been draining your battery." |
| In medias res | Start inside the story or the action | "Ten minutes in, the client hung up." |
| Direct callout | Name exactly who this is for | "If you freelance and hate invoicing, this is for you." |
| Number list | A count, only if the content really has it | "Two things I wish I'd known before renting." |
| Visual first | The image is the hook; the line frames it | Show the result, then: "Here's how." |

Contrarian has the highest ceiling and the highest risk of sounding like bait; investigator and specific result are safer baselines. A question hook is weak unless the question is sharp and specific.

Real numbers only: the shapes above use placeholder figures. A hook with a number needs that number from the speaker or from verified research.

## Upgrading a weak hook

Try in order, stop when it works:

1. Cut the first three words (they're usually throat-clearing).
2. Add the specific noun or real number.
3. Name the viewer.
4. Use a stronger, plainer verb.
5. Add the contradiction.
6. Turn it into a withheld reveal.

Still flat after that? The angle is the problem, not the wording. Go back to the topic and find a sharper angle.

## Checklist (each hook)

- [ ] Starts on the first word: no greeting, no name, no "today I want to talk about".
- [ ] A specific noun or detail, not a general claim.
- [ ] Understandable by someone with zero context (if a stranger wouldn't get it, simplify).
- [ ] The topic's key words are said out loud in the first seconds (platforms index speech, and the viewer knows they're in the right place).
- [ ] Resolves its first beat by second 3.
- [ ] Works on mute (visual + text) and with eyes closed (verbal).
- [ ] The script actually delivers what it promises.
- [ ] Sounds like the speaker. A clickbait line in a calm speaker's mouth sounds fake.
- [ ] No worn-out line from the language file ("Stop scrolling", "Nadie te cuenta esto"...).

If the hook needs a sentence of setup before it works, that setup is the real hook. Rewrite from there.
